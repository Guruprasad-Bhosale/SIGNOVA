ABSTRACT  
 
Indian Sign Language (ISL) is a vital communication tool for over 2.7 million deaf and hard -
of-hearing individuals in India. Despite a hearing -impaired population exceeding 5 million, 
significant communication barriers persist in essential domains like edu cation and healthcare, 
contributing to unemployment rates above 75%. Existing technologies, often designed for 
American Sign Language (ASL), fail to interpret ISL's unique complexities, including two -
handed configurations, non -manual markers like facial ex pressions, and distinct regional 
syntactic variations. This research -oriented project aims to develop a system that processes live 
video feeds to detect and translate ISL sequences into Hindi or English, supporting both 
isolated words and continuous expres sions to minimize misinterpretation .  
The proposed methodology involves a hybrid deep learning pipeline. A computer vision 
module, specifically MediaPipe, is used for real -time landmark extraction from video feeds, 
capturing 21 hand keypoints and body pos e data. This spatiotemporal data is then fed into a 
fused architecture combining Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) and Transformer models. 
This hybrid approach is designed to analyze both the spatial configurations of handshapes and 
the temporal patterns  of sign sequences .  
The system will be trained on a custom -augmented ISL corpus, aggregated from public 
repositories like iSign and new custom recordings, to improve generalizability. Evaluation will 
be conducted using standard metrics, including mean av erage precision (mAP), frames -per-
second (FPS), and BLEU scores for translation accuracy. The project's objectives are to 
investigate this architecture's effectiveness, aiming for an mAP above 97% and BLEU scores 
exceeding 0.75, while simultaneously benchm arking a new public ISL video repository.  
This framework not only prototypes a functional bidirectional translator but also advances the 
theoretical understanding of culturally adaptive models for low -resource sign languages. The 
outcomes aim to provide em pirical contributions to human -computer interaction and computer 
vision , aligning with global accessibility standards under the UN Convention on the Rights of 
Persons with Disabilities and targeting a latency reduction of up to 40%.

CHAPTER 1  
INTRODUCTION  
 
1.1 Introduction to Work Done  
Sign language translators are systems designed to convert visual, gestural information into textual or 
spoken forms, thereby bridging communication gaps for deaf and hard -of-hearing communities. This 
project focuses specifically on Indian Sign Language (ISL), t he primary mode of communication for over 
2.7 million users in India. The core of this research -oriented project is the development of a system capable 
of processing live video feeds to detect and translate ISL sequences. The system is designed to support 
both isolated words and, more importantly, continuous expressions, which are crucial for minimizing 
misinterpretation during dynamic, real -time interactions. The final output will be translated into accessible 
Hindi or English equivalents.  
 
1.2 Motivation  
The motivation for this project is rooted in the persistent communication barriers faced by India's hearing -
impaired population, which exceeds 5 million people. A critical lack of access to qualified ISL 
interpreters —with estimates suggesting fewer than 5 % have access —severely impacts essential domains 
like education, healthcare, and legal services. This exclusion directly contributes to socio -economic 
disparity, evidenced by an unemployment rate above 75% among hearing -impaired individuals in India.  
While  technological solutions exist, they are predominantly ASL -centric and fail to address the unique 
linguistic properties of ISL, thereby exacerbating exclusion. This research aims to develop a culturally 
adaptive tool that specifically targets these gaps. B y aligning with global accessibility standards, such as 
those outlined in the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, this project justifies 
intervention by targeting underexplored areas and fostering digital inclusion.

1.3 Problem Statement  
 
The development of an accurate ISL translator faces significant technical and linguistic challenges that are 
not addressed by current -generation systems. The problem can be broken down into the following key 
areas:  
 Linguistic Comple xity:  ISL is a complete language with its own grammar and syntax, which is 
distinct from spoken languages and other sign languages like ASL. Key complexities include:  
o Two -Handed Configurations:  Many ISL signs require complex, coordinated movements 
of both hands, which are difficult to track and interpret simultaneously.  
o Non-Manual Markers (NMMs):  ISL grammar relies heavily on NMMs, such as facial 
expressions, head tilts, eye gaze, and body posture. These subtle cues can alter meaning, 
(e.g., transforming a statement into a question).  
o Syntactic and Regional Variations:  ISL often uses a Topic -Comment structure, unlike 
the Subject -Verb -Object structure of English. Furthermore, it has significant regional 
dialects (e.g., urban Delhi vs. rural Tamil Nadu variants ) that models must learn to 
generalize.  
 Technical Hurdles:  
o Co-articulation and Transitions:  In continuous, fluid signing, signs blend together (co -
articulation), and models must distinguish meaningful signs from the transitional 
movements between them.  
o Environmental Variability:  A real -world system must be robust to diverse signing styles, 
speeds, and real -world challenges like poor lighting, cluttered backgrounds, and partial 
hand or body occlusion.  
o Dataset Scarcity:  Deep learning models require massive, high -quality, annotated video 
datasets. For low -resource languages like ISL, such datasets are critically scarce, which 
hinders model training and generalization.

1.4 Research Objectives  
 
Based on the identified problem statement, this project aims to achieve the following research objectives, 
as outlined in the synopsis:  
1. Investigate a hybrid CNN -Transformer architecture  for continuous ISL recognition. This 
involves evaluating its performance on augment ed datasets with the goal of achieving a mean 
average precision (mAP) above 97% to quantify improvements over static baselines.  
2. Evaluate bidirectional translation mechanisms , supporting ISL -to-text/speech and the reverse. 
This will be measured through empi rical trials with 15 -20 diverse signers, aiming for BLEU 
(Bilingual Evaluation Understudy) scores exceeding 0.75 to assess syntactic and cultural accuracy.  
3. Develop and benchmark a public ISL video repository  that surpasses 1,000 unique samples. 
This contri bution aims to address the dataset scarcity gap and measure generalizability for future 
accessibility research publications.

CHAPTER 2  
LITERATURE REVIEW  
 
2.1 Conceptual Overview  
Explorations into ISL translation have seen rapid advancements with the advent of deep lea rning, 
particularly for gesture detection. Early research efforts prioritized high -accuracy detection of individual, 
static gestures (e.g., alphabets, numbers) and isolated words. These systems often employed Convolutional 
Neural Networks (CNNs) combined w ith hand -tracking tools like MediaPipe.  
More recently, research has shifted towards tackling the more complex challenge of continuous and 
dynamic sign recognition, which requires modeling spatiotemporal dynamics. This has led to the 
integration of recurrent architectures like LSTMs (Long Short -Term Memory) and GRUs (Gated 
Recurrent Units), as well as transformer -based models (e.g., VideoMAE, TimeSformer) that use attention 
mechanisms to process video sequences. Despite progress, persistent issues remain, including robust 
support for continuous si gning, handling regional dialects, and managing the computational overhead of 
these advanced models.  
 
2.2 Review of Existing Methodologies  
To inform the gaps in real -time, bidirectional systems, this review examines several recent studies from 
2024 -2025, c ategorizing them by their scope and methodology.  
2.2.1 Static and Isolated Sign Recognition  
These systems focus on classifying discrete signs, forming the foundation for more complex models.  
 CNN -Based Static Detection (2025):  One study details a CNN -based system to detect 35 static 
ISL gestures. It uses sequential convolutional layers, ReLU activation, batch normalization, and 
MediaPipe for hand tracking. Trained on over 42,000 images, it achieved an exceptional 99.95% 
accuracy, outperforming baselines like  VGG16. While robust to lighting, its focus is limited to 
static alphabets and digits, with no support for continuous sequences.  
 Machine Learning Baselines (2025):  Another approach used traditional ML models, such as 
Random Forest with ORB/SIFT descriptors  and SVM classifiers, to achieve 98.44% accuracy on 
a 35-gesture dataset. These models are lightweight and deployable on standard CPUs. However, 
they are highly sensitive to lighting conditions and are limited to alphabets and numbers, neglecting 
full synt actic translation.  
 Recurrent Nets for Alphanumeric ISL (2025):  A hybrid model using LSTM -GRU sequences 
with optimized CNNs was developed to handle alphanumeric ISL from a 50,000 -image set. It 
achieved 99.4% accuracy on benchmarks and enabled multilingual speech output. Its primary 
limitation is its restriction to dis crete classes, lacking the ability to process continuous sign flow.

 MediaPipe -CNN -LSTM (2024):  This fusion model was designed to recognize specific 
emergency -related words. It achieved 98% accuracy at 25 FPS, showing robustness to occlusion.

2.2.2 Continuous and Dynamic Sign Recognition  
These more advanced systems attempt to interpret fluid, connected sign sequences.  
 YOLOv10 -ST (2025):  This framework integrates Swin Transformer blocks  into the YOLOv10 
object detection model for multi -scale ISL gesture detection. It achieved 97.62% mAP on static 
signs and an impressive 48.7 FPS on an RTX 3080 GPU. Its self -attention mechanism provides 
excellent occlusion robustness, making it ideal for mobile translators. Its main drawbacks are a 
modest dynamic video corpus (35 videos), risking overfitting, and a lack of full temporal 
sequencing for extended dialogues.  
 VideoMAE & Transformers (2025):  A comparative study evaluated models like VideoMAE, 
TimeSformer, and CNN -BiLSTM for isolated ISL word recognition. VideoMAE, using masked 
autoencoders, achieved a leading 96% accuracy. While benchmarking was standardized, the 
training was highly GPU -intensive (up to 9,964 seconds) and the scope was restricted  to single 
words, overlooking co -articulation.  
 CNN -LSTM -Transformer Pipeline (2025):  A vision -based pipeline was developed to translate 
both isolated and continuous ISL into English. It achieved 96.1% classification accuracy and a 0.74 
BLEU score for sente nces. A significant con was the use of ASL -augmented data, which led to 
cultural mismatches and a 6.3% word error rate on rapid signs. Furthermore, the LSTM memory 
overhead limited its viability on low -end hardware.  
 Pose -GCN (2025):  This approach used Grap h Convolutional Networks (GCNs) on skeletal 
keypoints with a Transformer seq2seq model for continuous sentence translation. It is lightweight 
and achieved a 0.78 BLEU score. Its primary weakness is sensitivity to keypoint errors from 
occlusion and difficul ty with fine handshape distinctions.

2.3 Outcome of Literature Review  
The literature survey reveals a clear trend: while static sign recognition has achieved impressive accuracy 
(often 95 -99%), this performance does not translate to the far more complex domain of continuous, 
dynamic sign language translation. The review highlights several critical gaps that this project will address:  
1. Continuous Signing Support:  This remains the most significant open c hallenge. Most high -
accuracy models are limited to isolated words or alphabets. Models that do attempt continuous 
translation struggle with co -articulation, temporal sequencing, and high error rates.  
2. Dataset Scarcity:  Progress is fundamentally bottlenecked  by the lack of large -scale, high -quality, 
and richly annotated ISL video datasets. Many studies use small, private datasets (e.g., 35 videos 
), which limits generalizability and risks overfitting.  
3. Computational and Hardware Limits:  Many promising models ( e.g., VideoMAE, LSTM 
pipelines) are computationally expensive, requiring high -end GPUs for training and inference, or 
suffering from memory overhead. This limits their practical deployment on real -time or edge 
devices.  
4. Cultural and Linguistic Nuance:  The u se of ASL -augmented data introduces cultural mismatches 
and translation errors. Existing technologies largely overlook ISL's unique topic -comment 
structure and regional dialects, which is a key focus of this project.  
This review confirms the need for hybri d architectures, like the proposed CNN -Transformer, which can 
balance precision and temporal analysis. Furthermore, it underscores the necessity of building new, 
augmented, and culturally -specific ISL resources to elevate research impact.

CHAPTER 3  
METHODOLOGY AND FRAMEWORK  
 
3.1 Proposed Methodology  
To address the gaps identified in the literature, this project proposes a hybrid deep learning pipeline. This 
"how" of the project is designed to be robust, efficient, and culturally adaptive, moving beyond simple 
static sign recognition to handle continuous, dynamic ISL sequences. The methodology fuses advanced 
computer vision for feature extraction with deep learning architectures optimized for spatiotemporal 
analysis. The entire system will b e trained on a custom -augmented ISL corpus to ensure it handles real -
world variability and low -resource constraints.  
3.2 System Architecture  
The proposed system architecture is a multi -stage pipeline designed for real -time processing of live video 
feeds.  
1. Input and Landmark Extraction:  The pipeline begins with a live video feed from a standard 
webcam. This feed is processed by a computer vision module powered by MediaPipe . This module 
performs high -fidelity, real -time tracking to extract crucial landmarks, including 21 hand keypoints 
and 33 body keypoints. Using MediaPipe provides robustness against common environmental 
challenges like occlusion and varying lighting.  
2. Spatiotemporal Analysis:  The sequence of extracted landmark data is fed into a hybrid deep 
learning model.  
o CNN Backbone:  A Convolutional Neural Network (CNN), inspired by efficient 
architectures like EfficientNet , processes the spatial information from the landmarks to 
analyze intricate hand configurations and poses.  
o Transformer Decoder:  The output features from the CNN are passed to a Transformer  
architecture. The transformer's self -attention mechanism analyzes the temporal movement 
patterns, learning the relationships between signs across the entire sequence to understand 
context and gr ammar.  
3. Translation and Output:  The final layer of the transformer outputs a sequence of recognized ISL 
signs.  
o ISL-to-Text:  This sequence is passed to a sequence -to-sequence (seq2seq)  model, which 
translates the sign sequence into its syntactically correct English or Hindi equivalent.  
o Text -to-Speech (TTS):  The generated text is then fed into a Google TTS  API to produce 
a multilingual speech output.

4. User Interface:  A Streamlit  interface will be developed to provide a user -friendly application for

3.3 Algorithms and Techniques  
The imple mentation will leverage several key technologies and algorithms:  
 Core Models:  
o MediaPipe:  For efficient and robust pose and hand landmark extraction.  
o Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN):  An EfficientNet -inspired backbone will be used 
for spatial feature ext raction.  
o Transformer:  A transformer decoder will be used for temporal sequence modeling, 
leveraging attention to capture long -range dependencies in sign language.  
 Training and Implementation:  
o Framework:  The system will be implemented in PyTorch . 
o Data Augmentation:  OpenCV  will be used to apply augmentations (e.g., flips, rotations, 
brightness changes) to the corpus to improve model robustness and generalize against 
varied illuminations.  
o Optimization:  The model will be trained using the Adam optimizer  with focal loss  to 
handle class imbalances.  
o Transfer Learning:  To accelerate training, the CNN backbone will be initialized using 
transfer learning  from weights pre -trained on the Kinetics dataset.  
 Evaluation Metrics:  
o Detection:  Mean Average Precision (mAP) and F1 -Score.  
o Translation:  BLEU (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy) score.  
o Performance:  Latency and Frames -Per-Second (FPS).

3.4 Implementation Plan  
The project is structured into five distinct phases over a 15 -week timeline, incorporating iterative 
validati on and ethical data handling.  
 Phase 1: Gap Refinement and Corpus Assembly (Weeks 1 -4)  
o Tasks:  Conduct a comprehensive literature synthesis to finalize research gaps. Aggregate 
an initial corpus of 800+ ISL clips from sources like iSign and custom recording s with 10 
diverse signers.  
o Data Handling:  Apply OpenCV augmentations and MediaPipe for keypoint extraction. 
Secure anonymized consents for all custom data. Partition data into 70/15/15 (train/val/test) 
splits.  
o Milestone:  Preprocessed dataset ready for base line training.  
 Phase 2: Architecture Prototyping (Weeks 5 -6)  
o Tasks:  Construct the hybrid CNN -Transformer architecture. Initialize with Kinetics pre -
trained weights.  
o Training:  Train sequentially, first on static subsets (50 epochs) and then escalating to 
dynamic sequences. Use TensorBoard for convergence tracking.  
o Milestone:  A functional hybrid model achieving >95% preliminary accuracy on validation 
data.  
 Phase 3: Translation Pipeline Integration (Weeks 7 -9)  
o Tasks:  Incorporate the seq2seq model for ISL -to-text translation. Append Google TTS for 
speech output. Develop the bidirectional (text -to-ISL) mapping.  
o Interface:  Prototype a Streamlit interface for live webcam trials on a set of 60 common 
phrases.  
o Milestone:  A working bidirectional application prototyp e. 
 Phase 4: Assessment (Weeks 10 -12)  
o Tasks:  Conduct rigorous evaluation. Compute mAP, F1 -score, BLEU, and latency across 
varied scenarios (e.g., occluded hands, fast signing).  
o Validation:  Solicit qualitative usability feedback from DHH (Deaf and Hard of Hearing) 
volunteers. Conduct t -tests against surveyed baselines.  
o Milestone:  A comprehensive metrics report and t -test results.

CONCLUSIONS  
 
4.1 Conclusion  
This project addresses the critical communication gap faced by over 5 million hearing -impaired individuals 
in India by developing a bidirectional ISL translator. The literature review confirmed that while deep 
learning has enabled high -precision static sign recognition (often >97%), significant research gaps persist 
in handling continuous signing, dataset scarcity, and the linguistic nuances of ISL. Existing systems, often 
ASL-centric, fail to manage ISL's unique grammar and regional variations, perpetuating digital exclusion.  
To bridge these gaps, this projec t proposes a hybrid CNN -Transformer architecture. This methodology 
leverages MediaPipe for robust landmark extraction and combines the spatial analysis power of CNNs 
with the temporal modeling capabilities of Transformers. The planned work includes the cre ation of an 
augmented 1,000+ sample video repository and a phased implementation plan to build and rigorously test 
the system. The objective is to deliver a prototype that not only achieves high accuracy (mAP > 97%, 
BLEU > 0.75) but also reduces translatio n latency by up to 40%.  
This framework moves beyond a simple translation tool; it serves as a research contribution to advance 
the theoretical understanding of culturally adaptive models for low -resource sign languages, aligning with 
UN accessibility goals  and paving the way for more inclusive technology.  
 
4.2 Future Scope  
While this project establishes a robust foundation, future work can expand upon its contributions. The 
primary avenue for extension is the continued expansion and diversification of the d ataset, moving well 
beyond the 1,000 -sample goal to include more signers, topics, and regional dialects to improve linguistic 
equity. Further research could focus on optimizing the hybrid model for deployment on edge devices, such 
as mobile phones, which w ould drastically increase accessibility. Additionally, refining the non -manual 
marker detection (facial expressions, eye gaze) remains a complex challenge that, if solved, would 
significantly enhance the system's grammatical accuracy and contextual underst anding.
